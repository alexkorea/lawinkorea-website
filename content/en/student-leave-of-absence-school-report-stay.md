If you take a leave of absence or miss the registration period, you may worry: "I heard the school tells immigration, so what happens to my visa?" In short, the school head makes the report within 15 days, and the parts that actually affect a student are the stay extension and any change of school or program.

## Key points

- When a student with a study or training status has not registered or has taken leave, or when study ends through dismissal, suspension of training or disappearance, the school head must report within 15 days (Article 19-4(2)).
- The starting date depends on the reason. For non-registration it is the day after the registration deadline, for leave it is the leave date, for dismissal or suspension it is that day, and for disappearance it is the day the school learns of it (Enforcement Decree, Article 24-8(1)).
- The Ministry of Justice residence manual restricts stay extensions for people who took leave for personal reasons or because of insufficient credits.
- If a student stops studying through course completion, leave or dismissal, moving down to a lower degree program is also restricted.

## 1. In which cases does the school report?

Article 19-4 of the Immigration Act asks the head of a school attended by an international student with a study or training status for two things. One is to designate a staff member in charge of managing international students and notify the immigration office. The other is to report within 15 days when any of the following occurs.

1. An admitted student, or one with training permission, has not registered by the registration deadline of each semester or has taken leave
2. Study or training has ended for reasons such as dismissal, suspension of training or disappearance

The report can also be made through an information network. Separately, the school head handles attendance and credit management, counseling to prevent students from leaving their status, and notifying the immigration office of the current situation (Enforcement Decree, Article 24-8(2)).

## 2. When does the 15-day count start?

Enforcement Decree Article 24-8(1) sets a separate starting date for each case.

| Reason | Day the 15 days start |
|---|---|
| Did not register by the semester's registration deadline | The day after the registration deadline |
| Leave of absence | The leave date |
| Dismissal, or study or training discontinued | The day of dismissal or discontinuation |
| Discontinued for a reason the school did not know, such as disappearance | The day the school learned of it |

For example, if a student has not registered by the deadline for the September semester, the school must report within 15 days starting the day after the deadline. That is why a school's report can be filed even right after a student submits leave paperwork.

## 3. Who is penalized if the report is missing or late?

The administrative fine falls on the school that carries the reporting duty. Article 100(1), item 2 of the Act makes a person who violates Article 19-4 subject to the fine, and Enforcement Decree Annex 2 divides it by how late the report is: KRW 100,000 (under 3 months), KRW 300,000 (3 months to under 6 months), KRW 500,000 (6 months to under 12 months), KRW 1,000,000 (1 year to under 2 years) and KRW 2,000,000 (2 years or more) (as of November 2026, Enforcement Decree Annex 2).

What matters to you as a student is not the school's fine but **how your stay is reviewed after the report is received**.

## 4. What changes in the stay review after leave or dismissal

According to the Ministry of Justice residence manual (the guide by status for residence applications), the following changes apply.

- **Stay extension**: Under the basic D-2 (study) extension principle, leave for housework or to postpone graduation is not recognized, and a person who suspended studies (took leave) for personal reasons or insufficient credits faces restricted extension. An exception is considered only when an unavoidable reason such as illness or an accident is recognized. Language training (D-4-1, D-4-7) is stated in the same spirit.
- **Moving to a lower degree program**: When changing schools, there is an exception allowing a move to a lower degree program if you meet requirements such as Korean ability (TOPIK Level 3 or higher), but the move is restricted if you stopped studying through completion, leave or dismissal.
- **Leave exception for internships**: For a D-2 student on a regional-type visa who meets all the requirements and takes leave for an internship, the manual provides that they may continue to stay without a separate summons even if a change report is received. This exception applies only to people who meet every requirement (completed semesters, Korean and grade standards, a maximum of one year per leave period, and so on).

In other words, even for the same leave, the review differs greatly depending on whether the reason is illness or an accident, personal circumstances or an internship. It is wise to gather documents proving the reason, such as a medical certificate or an internship contract, before you go on leave.

## 5. If you are taking leave, prepare like this

1. **Check your stay expiry date first.** If the expiry date passes during your leave, the reason for the leave becomes a subject of review at the extension application. If you miss the extension window, a separate violation problem can arise, as explained in [what to do if you missed your stay extension](/en/blog/overstay-penalty-extension-missed).
2. **Prepare documents proving the reason for leave.** Objective materials such as a hospital certificate, accident-related documents or an internship contract are best.
3. **Match the report details with the school's international office.** Make sure the leave date the school reports and the dates on the documents you submit do not conflict.
4. **Respond within the deadline if immigration contacts you.** If you received a summons, also check [your rights when you receive a summons](/en/blog/immigration-summons-statement-rights).
5. **Check the permitted scope before working a side job during leave.** Part-time work needs separate permission. The detailed standards are in [student part-time work permit requirements](/en/blog/student-part-time-work-permit-hours).
