Travelers who come to Korea are sometimes asked to show a passport at hotel or guesthouse check-in, and people who run lodging businesses wonder how to handle foreign guests' information. Article 81-3 of the Immigration Act applies this duty only in very special cases. This guide covers who must do what and when, and the fines, by article.

## Key points

- A foreigner with a short-stay status (stay of 90 days or less) is a **lodging foreigner** (Article 81-3(1), Article 10-2(1), item 1).
- The foreigner's duty to provide data is **limited to cases set by Ministry of Justice rule**, such as an infectious disease or terror alert.
- The operator must submit the data through an information network **within 12 hours** of receiving it (or of the alert) (Article 81-3(2)).
- Breach can bring a **fine of up to KRW 500,000** (as of October 2026, Article 100(3), items 1-2 and 1-3).

## 1. Who is covered?

A **lodging foreigner** under Article 81-3(1) is a foreigner with a **short-stay status** under Article 10-2(1), item 1, meaning a status to stay for tourism, visits and similar for 90 days or less (or longer where a visa waiver agreement allows). Long-stay status holders are not lodging foreigners under this provision. A lodging operator is one of the following.

- A person who **notified a lodging business** under the Public Health Control Act
- A person registered under the Tourism Promotion Act for **tourist lodging, urban homestay for foreign tourists or hanok experience** business

## 2. When must data be provided?

This is not a standing duty. Article 81-3(1) limits it to **cases set by Ministry of Justice rule**, such as an infectious disease crisis alert or terror alert. Under Enforcement Rule Article 69-2(1) these are:

1. An infectious disease crisis alert at the **'caution' level or above**, where the Minister of Justice finds the data necessary, for example after a request from the Minister of Health and Welfare or the KDCA, and issues a **notice**
2. A terror alert at the **'attention' level or above**, where the Minister of Justice finds the data necessary, for example after a request from a relevant agency, and issues a **notice**

The data to provide is one of the **passport, travel document or entry permit** (paragraph 2). To know whether the duty applies now, check the Ministry of Justice notice and the lodging's guidance.

## 3. How does the operator submit?

Under Article 81-3(2), the operator submits the data a foreigner provides to the Minister of Justice through the designated information network **within 12 hours** of the stay or the alert. Enforcement Rule Article 69-3 provides:

- The data are **copies** of the passport, travel document or entry permit.
- The operator **registers as a user** on the lodging network and sends electronic signature information with each submission.
- If fire, power failure or network failure makes the network unusable as recognized by the Minister, or maintenance is announced, submit by **email, fax, text, phone or in person**.

The operator holds and manages the data under the Personal Information Protection Act (Article 81-3(3)).

## 4. What if the duty is breached?

Under Article 100(3), a fine of up to KRW 500,000 applies to (as of October 2026):

- A lodging foreigner who **does not provide** data (item 1-2)
- An operator who **does not submit or submits falsely** (item 1-3)

The Enforcement Decree Annex 2 standards for both are (as of October 2026):

| Violations | Standard fine |
|---|---|
| 1st | KRW 100,000 |
| 2nd | KRW 200,000 |
| 3rd | KRW 300,000 |
| 4th | KRW 400,000 |
| 5th or more | KRW 500,000 |

The office head imposes and collects the fine (Article 100(4)), and the Minister of Justice may waive it considering the circumstances (Article 100(5)). If you receive a fine notice, a similar procedure is described in the [fine notice response guide](/en/blog/immigration-fine-notice-response-2026). Amounts for other violations are in the [overview of immigration penalties](/en/blog/immigration-law-penalties-guide).

## 5. Checklist by situation

**If you are a traveler**

1. Check that you have your passport at check-in. Foreigners also have a duty to carry a passport or similar document (Article 27).
2. Ask the lodging whether the request is because of a special alert period.

**If you are a lodging operator**

1. Check whether a Ministry of Justice notice currently applies the duty.
2. Register as a user on the lodging network in advance.
3. Record the 12-hour deadline and confirm the substitute submission method for network failures.
4. Manage collected data under the Personal Information Protection Act.
