Sometimes someone asks you to open a phone line or a bank account, and you hand over your alien registration card thinking, "It's only for a moment." In short, Article 33-3 of the Immigration Act prohibits giving your own card to someone when you know it will be used improperly, and also using another person's card or registration number. Breaking it can lead to criminal punishment and a review for deportation.

## Key points

- Article 33-3 lists acts that "no person" may commit. It applies to both foreigners and Koreans.
- The item 1 act, where an employer holds a passport or ID card, is covered in a [separate guide](/en/blog/passport-id-card-retention-ban).
- A violation can mean up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (as of November 2026, Article 94, item 19).
- If a foreigner violates it, it is also a ground for deportation (Article 46(1), item 12-2).

## 1. Which acts are prohibited?

Items 2 through 5 of Article 33-3 are as follows.

- **Item 2**: Falsely generating a foreigner registration number and using it, or brokering its use, for your own or another person's money or financial gain
- **Item 3**: Passing on, distributing or brokering a program that falsifies a registration number or a mobile alien registration card
- **Item 4**: Improperly using another person's alien registration card or mobile alien registration card, giving your own card to another person while knowing it will be used improperly, and brokering either act
- **Item 5**: Improperly using, or brokering the use of, another person's foreigner registration number for your own or another person's money or financial gain

The situation seen most often in daily life falls under item 4. Both the person who lends the card and the person who borrows it are caught by the same item. A mobile alien registration card is treated the same as the physical card.

## 2. What are the penalties?

Violating Article 33-3 can lead to up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million under Article 94, item 19 (as of November 2026, Article 94, item 19).

In practice, some cases end after the local immigration office investigates and notifies a penalty fine (beomchikgeum) equivalent to a criminal fine (Article 102(1)). Enforcement Rules Annex 7 sets the standard penalty fine for violations of Article 33-3 (excluding item 1) at KRW 10 million for the 1st, KRW 15 million for the 2nd, KRW 20 million for the 3rd, and KRW 30 million for the 4th or more. The count is the number of times you received a penalty fine for the same violation within the last 3 years (as of November 2026, Enforcement Rules Annex 7).

The standard amount can be reduced or increased by up to one half after considering age, circumstances, motive and result of the violation, ability to pay and similar factors (Enforcement Rules, Article 86(2)).

On the other hand, if the investigation finds that the circumstances warrant imprisonment or heavier, the case can be reported to prosecutors immediately, without any notice (Article 102(3)).

## 3. Deportation and the permanent resident exception

A foreigner who violates Article 33-3 falls under a ground for deportation under Article 46(1), item 12-2. However, a person with permanent resident status (F-5) is in principle not deported under Article 46(2), and the listed exceptions (such as insurrection and treason offenses) do not include a violation of Article 33-3. The permanent resident exception is explained in detail in [Can permanent residents be deported?](/en/blog/permanent-resident-deportation-exception)

Even after you pay a penalty fine, residence-related decisions are judged separately. You can see how a fine record may affect the next residence permit review in the [guide on responding to a fine notice](/en/blog/immigration-fine-notice-response-2026).

## 4. Common misunderstandings

- **"It's fine because it's family or a friend"**: The law looks not at the relationship but at whether you gave the card while knowing it would be used improperly. If you handed it over without knowing who would use it or where, the situation may differ, but if you knew the purpose, you can fall under the same item.
- **"It's my card, so I can do what I want"**: Giving even your own card to someone while knowing it will be used improperly is prohibited.
- **"I only shared the number"**: Improperly using a registration number for financial gain falls under item 5. It can be a problem even without the physical card.
- **"I'll just say I lost it"**: If it was lost, you simply go through the reissue procedure. How to get a reissue is covered in the [guide on carrying and reissuing a lost card](/en/blog/foreigner-id-card-carry-lost-reissue).

If the card is also used for a phone line, a bank account, a job or another purpose, separate financial or telecommunications laws may apply. The risk of being drawn into voice phishing is covered in the [guide on voice phishing penalties for foreigners](/en/blog/voice-phishing-foreigners).

## 5. If you have already handed it over or borrowed one

1. **First work out who has it now and what it was used for.** Write down the dates, the other person and where it was used.
2. **Get it back if you can, and apply for reissue if it was lost or stolen.** As long as the card is in someone else's hands, the risk continues.
3. **If you received a summons or a notice, check the deadline first.** A penalty fine must be paid within 15 days of receiving the notice (Article 105(1)), and if you received a summons, also read the [guide on responding to a summons](/en/blog/immigration-summons-statement-rights).
4. **Prepare explanatory documents that stick to the facts.** You need evidence to support how it happened and that there was no intent. Statements that differ from the facts can make things worse.
