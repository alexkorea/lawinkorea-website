Some cruise travelers step off at a port city to look around and then miss the return time, or wander outside the permitted area into another city. In short, cruise passengers may land only within a tourist landing permit and crew only within a crew landing permit. Landing without a permit, or breaking conditions such as the period or the permitted area, can lead to criminal punishment and a deportation review.

## Key points

- Cruise passengers may land for up to 3 days under a tourist landing permit, and crew for up to 15 days under a crew landing permit (Immigration Act, Article 14(1) and Article 14-2(1)).
- A tourist landing permit lapses the moment the ship the passenger disembarked from departs (Enforcement Decree, Article 18-3(4)).
- Landing without a permit or breaking its conditions is punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (as of November 2026, Article 94, items 5 and 6).
- Both are also listed as grounds for deportation (Article 46(1), items 6 and 7).

## 1. How do the tourist landing permit and the crew landing permit differ?

Both systems let a person leave the ship briefly, separately from a visa or an ordinary entry permission.

| Item | Crew landing permit | Tourist landing permit |
|---|---|---|
| Article | Article 14 | Article 14-2 |
| Who | Foreign crew members | Foreign passengers on a passenger ship making international tourist circuits |
| Applicant | Master of the ship, carrier, or the person | Master of the ship or carrier |
| Period | Up to 15 days | Up to 3 days |
| Conditions | Period, restrictions on the permitted area and so on may be attached | The crew landing permit provisions apply mutatis mutandis |

When a permit is granted, a landing permit document is issued. It can carry conditions such as the permitted period and a restriction on the permitted area (Article 14(3)). The head of the regional immigration office may extend the period if needed (Article 14(5)), and the same rule applies to passengers' tourist landing permits (Article 14-2(3)). The provision on providing fingerprints and facial information applies as under entry screening (Article 12-2), with an exception where providing them is difficult in the procedure.

## 2. Which passengers can get a permit?

Enforcement Decree Article 18-3 sets the standards for a tourist landing permit as follows.

- The passenger must hold their own valid passport.
- The passenger must disembark for tourism and **plan to return to the ship they disembarked from, at the port of call where they disembarked, and leave Korea**.
- The passenger must be a person designated by the Minister of Justice, such as someone who can enter without a visa under a visa waiver agreement.

A permit is not granted to a passenger who is subject to an entry ban or refusal. In addition, if the cruise line is found not to have managed its passengers properly, for example because the share of past permit holders who did not return to the ship exceeds the set standard, permits for that ship itself may be restricted (Enforcement Decree, Article 18-3(3)). In other words, one person who strays can affect other passengers' chances to go ashore.

## 3. What happens if you miss the ship or leave the permitted area?

A tourist landing permit lapses the moment the ship the passenger disembarked from departs. This is so even where the landing period has been extended (Enforcement Decree, Article 18-3(4)). So if you miss the departure time and remain in Korea, you can end up staying without a permit.

The penalties set by law by type of violation are as follows.

- **Landing without a permit** (Article 94, item 5): a person who landed without a crew landing permit or a tourist landing permit.
- **Violating the permit conditions** (Article 94, item 6): a person who broke conditions written in the permit, such as the period or the permitted area.

In both cases the penalty is up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (as of November 2026, Article 94, items 5 and 6).

## 4. What are the standard penalty fine amounts?

Depending on the investigation, immigration offenses may be handled by notifying a penalty fine (beomchikgeum) (Article 102(1)). Enforcement Rules Annex 7 sets the standard amounts for landing without a permit by number of violations: KRW 2 million for the 1st, KRW 5 million for the 2nd, KRW 15 million for the 3rd, and KRW 30 million for the 4th or more. For breaking the permit conditions it sets KRW 1 million for the 1st, KRW 5 million for the 2nd, KRW 15 million for the 3rd, and KRW 30 million for the 4th or more (as of November 2026, Enforcement Rules Annex 7).

The count here is the number of times you received a penalty fine disposition for the same violation within the last 3 years. The standard amount may be reduced or increased by up to one half, taking into account age, circumstances, the motive and result of the violation, ability to pay and so on (Enforcement Rules, Article 86(2)).

## 5. Deportation and entry restrictions

A person who landed without a permit falls under Article 46(1), item 6, and a person who broke the permit conditions falls under item 7 of the same paragraph, both as grounds for deportation. If deportation or a departure measure is carried out, later entry may be restricted. The length of an entry ban and how to lift it are covered in our [entry ban removal guide](/en/blog/entry-ban-removal) and [voluntary departure guide](/en/blog/foreigner-voluntary-departure-2026).

An attempt to land without a permit is also punishable (Article 99(1)). There is also a special rule exempting punishment for a refugee who entered or landed directly from a place of persecution and reports the violation in person without delay (Article 99-2).

## 6. Steps to take if something goes wrong after you go ashore

1. **Check the departure time and your permit.** The permitted period and area written on the permit are the standard.
2. **If you missed the ship, tell the cruise line and the nearest immigration office right away.** The longer you wait, the harder it is to explain.
3. **Keep your passport and landing permit safe.**
4. **Organize your circumstances and prepare explanatory materials.** Set out reasons such as transport delays or illness, with evidence, truthfully.
5. **If you have questions about other entry screening situations,** see also our [guide to airport entry refusal and conditional entry permits](/en/blog/conditional-entry-permit-guide).
