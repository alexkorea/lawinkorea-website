You may be receiving treatment after an injury at work, in a dispute with your employer over wages, or in the middle of a lawsuit, while the end of your permitted stay draws near. If your current status no longer lets you stay, Miscellaneous (G-1) status may be an option. G-1 is a temporary status that lasts until the situation is resolved, so eligibility, periods and whether you can work differ by type.

## Key points

- G-1 is granted to people who do not fit another status and are **recognized by the Minister of Justice**. The maximum period at one time is **1 year**.
- Industrial accident and illness treatment are granted within 1 year; **lawsuits and unpaid wage mediation within 6 months**.
- The industrial accident, treatment, lawsuit and unpaid wage types are **not among those eligible for work permission** in the manual. Working without permission is a violation.
- Apply **before your current period of stay ends**.

## 1. Who can get G-1

The residence manual defines the scope of G-1 as activities that do not fall under Diplomat (A-1) through Marriage Migrant (F-6), Working Holiday (H-1) or Visiting Employment (H-2), and lists the people the Minister of Justice recognizes as:

- People claiming or being treated for industrial accidents, and their families (G-1-1)
- People being treated for illness or accidents, and their families (G-1-2)
- People involved in lawsuits (G-1-3)
- People in mediation at a labor office over unpaid wages (G-1-4)
- Refugee applicants (G-1-5) and rejected applicants granted humanitarian stay (G-1-6)
- People who need humanitarian consideration such as pregnancy or childbirth (G-1-9)
- Foreign patients (G-1-10) and people needing humanitarian consideration such as victims of sexual violence (G-1-11)
- Families of people who died in accidents, families of humanitarian stay holders, and other cases

## 2. Eligibility and periods by type

**Industrial accident (G-1-1)**: People with a pending claim or re-examination for industrial accident compensation, people hospitalized for an industrial accident or receiving care approved by the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service, people treating after-effects, and their spouses and lineal family. Stay is granted within 1 year until hospital treatment and compensation are complete; extensions are in principle within 6 months (within 1 year for serious cases).

**Illness or accident treatment (G-1-2)**: Registered foreigners who need long-term treatment for illness or an accident and cannot keep their existing status, and short-term visa holders who had an accident and need long-term treatment. Stay is granted within 1 year, with extensions of up to 6 months at a time. A person who came on a short-term visa for a health check or treatment and then needs long-term care changes to Foreign Patient (G-1-10) instead.

**Lawsuit (G-1-3)**: People pursuing civil suits such as damages for an industrial accident or return of a lease deposit, people in criminal trials while not detained (for example after bail or cancellation of detention), and people in family or administrative suits. Stay is granted within 6 months, and each extension is also within 6 months.

**Unpaid wages (G-1-4)**: People whose unpaid wage complaint to the Ministry of Employment and Labor is in mediation, or who filed a complaint that was not resolved and are now in a civil suit. Stay is granted within 6 months, with extensions within 6 months.

## 3. Documents

Common items are the application form, passport, one standard photo and the fee, plus documents proving the reason for each type.

- **Industrial accident**: compensation review claim or re-examination claim, medical certificate for the industrial accident, proof of relationship for family members, and a livelihood capacity review form
- **Illness or accident**: medical opinion or other proof of the need for treatment, proof of ability to pay for treatment and stay, and a letter of guarantee
- **Lawsuit**: copy of the complaint, certificate of filing, copy of a legal aid decision or other proof of the claim, and a letter of guarantee
- **Unpaid wages**: copy of the complaint filed with the labor ministry, the ministry's certificate of unpaid wages, and a letter of guarantee

For extensions, a G-1 status review form and proof of residence (lease, accommodation confirmation and so on) are added. Documents may be added or removed during intake.

## 4. Can you work?

G-1 covers activities outside other statuses, so to work you need separate permission for activities outside your status. The manual allows work permission for these types:

- Families of people who died in accidents, victims of sexual violence and similar, and other cases: simple labor outside restricted fields, up to 1 year
- Refugee applicants: those 6 months past their application and other cases, simple labor outside restricted industries such as construction
- Humanitarian stay holders and their families: the same scope as refugee applicants, but construction is allowed

The industrial accident, treatment, lawsuit and unpaid wage types are not on this list. Working without permission is a violation of the rules on activities outside your status; see the [guide to sanctions for unauthorized activities](/en/blog/unauthorized-activity-disposition-2026). If your workplace changes, you need new permission.

## 5. Timing and cautions

- **Apply before expiry.** Under the Immigration Act you must obtain a change of status in advance before engaging in another status's activities, and staying past your period is itself a violation. If you have already missed it, see the [overstay fine guide](/en/blog/overstay-penalty-extension-missed) first. The general change procedure is in the [change of status guide](/en/blog/status-change-permit-before-activity).
- **The period ends when the reason ends.** The period is based on how long treatment or the lawsuit continues, so keep collecting documents that show progress.
- **Changing to other statuses may be restricted.** The manual restricts G-1 holders (other than humanitarian stay holders and some others) from changing to Student (D-2), and in principle bars in-country change to Marriage Migrant (F-6) for people who received G-1 while overstaying or in similar situations.
- **Employment permit workers have a restoration route.** Foreign workers who switched to G-1 for industrial accident treatment and still have time left under their stay limit can follow the procedure to restore Non-professional Employment (E-9) status.
