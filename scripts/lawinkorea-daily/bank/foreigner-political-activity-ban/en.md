Foreigners living in Korea sometimes wonder whether they may join a protest or rally, or post political comments on social media. The Immigration Act in principle prohibits political activity by foreigners staying in Korea, and breach can lead to a stop order or even deportation. This guide covers Article 17 and related provisions. First, the Act sets no specific test for what is political activity, so each case needs its own judgment.

## Key points

- A foreigner staying in Korea **must not engage in political activity** except as this Act or other laws provide (Article 17(2)).
- The Minister of Justice may order in writing that the activity **stop**, or give other necessary orders (Article 17(3)).
- Violating Article 17(2) is a **ground for deportation** (Article 46(1), item 8).
- A permanent resident is not deported except in the cases the law lists (Article 46(2)).

## 1. Scope of the prohibited activity

Article 17(2) prohibits political activity but does not define it. Whether an act falls under it is judged from the specific facts, such as the content, purpose and manner of participation. This guide cannot say that a particular act is allowed or prohibited. The provision also says that cases provided in other laws are exceptions. If you have a concern before acting, it is safer to ask the immigration office for your place of residence.

## 2. Stop orders and compliance

Under Article 17(3), the Minister of Justice may order a foreigner who has engaged in political activity to stop, or give other necessary orders, in writing. Enforcement Decree Article 22 requires the stop order to state:

1. That the activity must **stop immediately**
2. That **deportation or other measures** will follow if the order is not obeyed
3. Other necessary matters

The order is issued directly by the Minister or through the office head to the foreigner. When an office head finds a person recognized as engaged in political activity, the head must report promptly to the Minister (Enforcement Rule, Article 26).

## 3. Deportation ground and procedure

Article 46(1), item 8 makes a person who violates Article 17(1) or (2) subject to deportation. A permanent resident, however, is not deported under Article 46(2) except in the cases the law lists, such as treason-type offenses. An immigration officer may investigate a person suspected of falling under a ground for deportation (Article 47). Rights and procedure in an investigation are in the [offense review guide](/en/blog/immigration-offense-review-guide).

Even where a ground in Article 46(1) appears to apply, a person who **wishes to leave voluntarily at their own expense** can be given a departure order (Article 68(1), item 1). If a person does not leave by the deadline, a deportation order may be issued and any deposit may be forfeited (Article 68(4)). The options are compared in the [voluntary departure guide](/en/blog/foreigner-voluntary-departure-2026).

## 4. How it differs from a restriction on scope of activity

Under Article 22, the Minister of Justice may restrict a foreigner's residence or scope of activity and set required conduct when necessary for public order or important national interests. Breaking such a restriction or condition can bring up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million under Article 94, item 14, and the Enforcement Rule Annex 7 standard fine is KRW 3 million for the first time (as of October 2026). Rules on activities beyond your status are in the [guide on activities outside status](/en/blog/unauthorized-activity-disposition-2026).

## 5. Checklist before and after acting

1. **Review your status and the activity.** Work out for yourself whether it has a political character.
2. **If unsure, ask the office before acting.** Keep a record of your question and the answer.
3. **If you receive a stop order, comply at once.** The order states that the activity must stop immediately and that deportation may follow otherwise.
4. **If you get a summons, check the schedule and purpose.** A summons states its purpose, date and place (Enforcement Decree, Article 58).
5. **Organize how you took part and to what extent.** It helps if you need to explain later.
