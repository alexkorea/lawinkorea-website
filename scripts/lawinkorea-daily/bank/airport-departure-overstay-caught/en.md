You realize late that your stay has expired, rush to the airport, and then hear "your period has passed" at departure immigration. It is a bad moment. The short answer first: overstaying is not on the list of grounds for a departure suspension. What does happen is that, once the overstay is found at departure screening, an immigration officer may investigate, and the case then goes one of three ways: a fine notice, a departure order or a deportation order. The office decides which, based on the facts of the case.

## Key points

- A foreigner must carry a valid passport and go through departure immigration by an immigration officer at the port of departure. An overstay found at this stage can start an investigation.
- The investigation is done as questioning, and your statement goes into a written record. You can ask for corrections before you sign.
- The disposition is mainly one of three: a fine notice, a departure order or a deportation order. If the offense looks serious enough, a criminal complaint is possible.
- A departure suspension is a separate system. It applies because of other grounds such as a criminal trial, an investigation or unpaid amounts, not because of the overstay.

## 1. What does departure immigration check?

When leaving, a foreigner must hold a valid passport and be examined by an immigration officer at the port of departure. Some foreigners can use automated departure screening, and officers may use the biometric data provided at entry in the departure check.

So even if you think you can slip out quietly, assume your period of stay may surface at this stage. The statute does not describe what exactly is said at the screening counter, so this guide sticks to the procedures the law sets out.

## 2. Once an overstay is found, questioning comes first

Immigration officers can investigate a foreigner suspected of falling under a ground for deportation. Staying on past the authorized period is on that list. The law is fairly specific about how the questioning works.

- Officers can require you to appear and question you, and another immigration officer must take part.
- Your statement is recorded. The record must be read to you or shown to you, and you must be asked whether anything is wrong. If you ask to add, delete or change something, that statement must be entered.
- You affix a seal across the pages and sign or stamp the record. If you cannot or refuse, that fact is written in the record.
- If you do not understand Korean, an interpreter must interpret, and statements in foreign writing are to be translated.
- Searching your residence or belongings, or requiring documents, needs your consent.

The step people miss most is checking the record. In a hurry many people just sign, but if the start date or reason of the overstay is written wrongly, it can affect the later disposition. Ask for an interpreter on anything unclear and point out corrections on the spot.

## 3. Path one: fine notice and temporary payment

If the investigation establishes the offense, the head of the office may notify you in writing, with reasons, to pay a fine. You must pay within 15 days of receiving the notice, and paying as notified means you are not punished again for the same case. If you do not pay in time, the office must file a criminal complaint.

For someone with a flight to catch, there is a temporary payment system. If you want to pay temporarily after a notified disposition, the head of the office may have you do so. You submit a temporary payment application, pay the fine and receive a receipt. Whether to allow it is up to the office head, so do not assume it is always available.

The amount depends on how long you overstayed. The base amount is 500,000 won for under one month and rises with the period, reaching 30 million won for seven years or more (as of October 2026, Enforcement Rules Article 86(2), Annex 7). The office head may reduce or increase it within one half of the base amount, taking into account age, circumstances, motive, ability to pay and number of violations. The bracket-by-bracket table is in [Overstay fines after a missed extension](/en/blog/overstay-penalty-extension-missed), so it is not repeated here.

## 4. Path two: departure order

A departure order can be issued to a person for whom leaving the country after a notified disposition is deemed appropriate, and also to a person who falls under a ground for deportation but wants to leave voluntarily at their own expense.

It is issued as a written departure order. Points to know:

- The departure deadline is set within 30 days from the date of issue.
- A restriction on residence or other conditions may be attached.
- If considered necessary, a deposit of up to 20 million won may be required (as of October 2026, Article 68(3)). The amount takes into account the reason and motive, prior violations, age, circumstances and assets, flight risk, and humanitarian reasons.
- If you do not leave by the deadline or breach a condition, a deportation order is issued without delay, and all or part of the deposit may be forfeited to the national treasury.
- A deposit that is not forfeited is returned when you leave.

If it is clear that you cannot leave within the deadline because of illness or another unavoidable reason, you can apply for a deferral by submitting a departure deadline deferral application with materials explaining the reason. It covers cases where leaving in time is clearly impossible.

## 5. Path three: deportation and criminal complaint

If the investigation finds a ground for deportation, the office head may issue a deportation order, and when issuing the order must tell you that you can file an objection with the Minister of Justice. If no ground is found, you must be told so without delay.

Separately, if the offense is found to warrant imprisonment or heavier, the office must file a complaint at once without a fine notice. There is also a rule that a person issued a deportation order is not reported for failing to pay the fine. Because the rules differ by path, the outcome can differ even for the same length of overstay.

## 6. How is this different from a departure suspension?

Searches mix up "blocked from leaving at the airport" and "caught for overstay," but they are different things. A departure suspension is imposed by the Minister of Justice on grounds such as a pending criminal trial, an unfinished prison sentence, unpaid fines, forfeitures or taxes above a set amount, or the need for a criminal investigation. Overstaying is not on that list.

A foreigner who could not leave by the authorized period because of such a suspension can leave without a separate extension procedure within 10 days after the suspension is lifted. Grounds and lifting are covered in the [departure suspension guide](/en/blog/foreigner-departure-suspension-guide).

## 7. What stays on record after you leave

Leaving is not the end. The record follows you when you come back or change status.

- A person who left after a deportation order and has not yet passed 5 years can be barred from entry. See the [entry ban and deportation guide](/en/blog/entry-ban-removal).
- Some status reviews, such as permanent residence, treat a person as disqualified if 7 years have not passed since leaving after a deportation order, or 5 years since leaving after a departure order.
- For a multiple-entry visa for frequent travelers, a person who within 3 years before applying received a notified disposition of 5 million won or more for overstay and the like, or a departure or deportation order, is excluded.

This is where paying a fine only differs most from getting a departure or deportation order. Check the type of disposition exactly on your paperwork.

## 8. What to prepare before and after the airport

1. Check that your passport is valid and when your period of stay ended. The length of the overstay decides the fine bracket.
2. If there were reasons for the delay or for wanting to stay on, gather evidence early, such as hospital records or documents showing a delay, and only what matches the facts.
3. Read the record to the end and ask for an interpreter for anything you do not understand.
4. Photograph the fine notice, departure order, temporary payment receipt and deposit receipt. You will need them later for the departure deadline and the return of the deposit.
5. If it looks like the matter is moving to a criminal complaint or investigation, consult someone who handles investigations and trials, not an administrative agent.
