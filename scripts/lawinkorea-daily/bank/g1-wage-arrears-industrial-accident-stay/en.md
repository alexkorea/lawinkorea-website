Maybe your wages have not arrived for months and you filed a complaint with the labor office. Maybe you were hurt at work and are receiving industrial accident care, and your authorized stay is about to end. If the visa runs out before the case does, you may have to leave before you receive the money or the compensation. The Other (G-1) status is the route for this situation: wage arrears fall under G-1-4 and industrial accidents under G-1-1.

## Key points

- **Wage arrears (G-1-4):** for people whose back-pay complaint is under mediation at the Ministry of Employment and Labor, or whose unresolved complaint has become a civil lawsuit. Granted within **6 months** at a time.
- **Industrial accident (G-1-1):** for people with an industrial accident compensation review or appeal pending, or receiving care, and their family. The first permit is within **1 year**; extensions are in principle within **6 months** (within 1 year for severely injured patients).
- Apply **before your current period of stay ends**. Missing the date creates a separate overstay problem.
- Neither type appears on the list of cases where permission to work is described, so **check before you take any job**.

## 1. What G-1 is

Other (G-1) is for people who do not fit any status from Diplomat (A-1) through Visit and Employment (H-2), or the permanent residence category, and who are recognized by the Minister of Justice. The maximum period granted at one time is 1 year. The recognized range includes people pursuing or receiving treatment after an industrial accident, with their family, and people whose wage arrears are under mediation at a labor agency.

The G-1 status has several types (medical treatment, lawsuits, refugee applicants and so on). This guide goes deeper on the two types that start from a workplace dispute.

## 2. Wage arrears complaint or lawsuit (G-1-4)

There are two eligible groups: people who filed a back-pay complaint with the Ministry of Employment and Labor and are under mediation, and people whose complaint was not resolved and who are now in a civil lawsuit.

For the first change of status you submit the application form, passport, one photo and the fee, plus:

- a copy of the complaint filed with the ministry
- the confirmation of unpaid wages issued by the ministry, or similar
- a letter of identity guarantee
- a living-ability review confirmation (used when the extension is reviewed)

One point where people often get stuck: **the complaint has been filed, but the confirmation of unpaid wages has not been issued.** Because the ministry issues it, ask when and how you can receive it at the time you file the complaint.

Extensions are also within 6 months at a time. People who received a grace period for departure get an extension within 3 months. For an extension you submit the confirmation of unpaid wages or a receipt from the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, lawsuit documents if you are in litigation, the G-1 qualification review confirmation form, an identity guarantee, and proof of where you live (lease, accommodation confirmation, utility receipts, dormitory fee receipts and so on).

## 3. Industrial accident claim or care (G-1-1)

Eligible are people who have applied for an industrial accident compensation review or an appeal, people hospitalized because of the accident or receiving care after approval from the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service, and people being treated for after-effects. A spouse and direct family members can apply with them. The first permit runs for the period needed to finish inpatient treatment and compensation, within 1 year.

First-time documents are the review or appeal request, a hospital diagnosis for the accident, family relationship documents for family members, and a living-ability review confirmation. The legal document list names a confirmation of industrial accident insurance benefits or a confirmation of the accident, and it does not require an identity guarantee from the injured person at the status change stage. The names of the documents differ slightly between sources, so ask the office which ones it accepts before you file.

Extensions are in principle within 6 months, within 1 year for severely injured patients. Extension documents are the hospital diagnosis, the treatment plan review decision notice issued by the compensation service (industrial accident insurance card) or an after-effects service card, the G-1 qualification review confirmation form, and proof of address.

## 4. A fork in the road before you choose G-1

If you hold a work status such as E-9, there is another route besides G-1: **changing workplace**. They have different purposes.

| Item | Workplace change | G-1 change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Move to another employer and keep working | Stay until the complaint, lawsuit or care ends |
| Can you work? | Start only after the new workplace is permitted | Not on the list of cases where permission to work is described |

A workplace change must be requested at the employment center within 1 month after the labor contract ends, and the immigration office's permission must come within 3 months of the request. If you could not apply or be permitted because of an industrial accident, illness and similar reasons, the period is counted from the day the reason ends. In that case, before your stay expires you apply for an extension, attaching the extension receipt issued by the employment center, industrial accident documents and a medical certificate, and the fee is waived.

Working conditions that differ from the contract, or unfair treatment by the employer that makes it hard to keep the contract, are also listed as reasons for a workplace change. Whether wage arrears fall under this depends on the facts and cannot be assumed. What you need to weigh separately is **whether to move to a new workplace while continuing the collection process (complaint or lawsuit), or to stay on G-1 until the case ends.**

## 5. Can you work?

This is the most common question, so here is the original wording in summary. The guidance lists permission to engage in work outside the status for family members of people who died in accidents (G-1-7), victims of sexual violence and similar cases (G-1-11), other eligible cases (G-1-99), refugee applicants, and humanitarian stay permit holders and their families. Wage arrears (G-1-4) and industrial accident (G-1-1) are not on that list.

Permission to work must be obtained in advance; it cannot be obtained after you start. The law also states that a foreigner who wants to work needs a status that allows employment. If you must work because of hardship, ask the competent office first.

## 6. After the case ends

- **If you want to return to E-9:** foreign workers who came under the employment permit system, changed to G-1 for treatment of an industrial accident and whose maximum period of stay has not been reached can use the E-9 status recovery procedure. The cap is counted from the date of entry: 3 years for E-9, or within 1 year 10 months if the employer re-hired the worker after 3 years.
- **If you want a different status:** the guidance for changes to Student (D-2) and language training says that G-1 holders, except some such as humanitarian stay permit holders, are restricted from changing status. Plan on the assumption that changing out of G-1 is not easy.
- **If you have to leave:** when the G-1 permit ends you must depart or find another way to stay. Going past the date creates a separate problem; see [our guide to overstay fines](/en/blog/overstay-penalty-extension-missed).

## 7. Order of steps

1. Check your current expiry date first.
2. Gather the documents: for wage arrears, the complaint receipt and the confirmation of unpaid wages; for an industrial accident, the diagnosis and the request (or care approval documents).
3. Apply for the change of status (G-1) before the date.
4. Before the G-1 period ends, apply for an extension with documents showing where your case stands.
5. If you need to work, check whether permission is possible before you start.
