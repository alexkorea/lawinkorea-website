Plenty of people sign an identity guarantee for a friend, family member or employee and only afterwards wonder, "What exactly did I just take on?" The short answer: the core of a guarantor's liability is the cost of the foreigner staying in Korea, being held in protection, and leaving the country. The law says the guarantor can be made to bear all or part of those costs, and if the guarantor fails to pay and the state ends up covering them, the state can claim the money back from the guarantor.

## Key points

- The guarantee form contains three promises: the foreigner will obey the law during the stay, you will bear departure travel expenses and related costs, and you will bear costs that arise during the stay or during protection.
- The guarantee period is capped at 4 years, and permit periods cannot exceed it.
- If a guarantor fails to meet the obligation and the state bears the cost, the state can claim reimbursement, and in some cases a deposit of up to 3 million won per foreigner can be required in advance (as of October 2026, Article 90(4)).
- I could not find any set procedure for withdrawing a guarantee. The law only says that withdrawal can lead to the foreigner's permits being cancelled or changed.

## What exactly do you promise when you sign?

The Ministry of Justice identity guarantee form has just three lines of guarantee content. The guarantor will make the foreigner comply with all laws during the stay. The guarantor will bear responsibility for payment of departure travel expenses and related costs. The guarantor will bear responsibility for payment of costs arising during the stay or during protection. Below that sits the sentence confirming that the guarantor has checked that the foreigner's identity is in order for the stay in Korea and guarantees the items above.

One common misunderstanding: people think they are liable without limit for whatever the foreigner does. The scope written in the form and the law is costs related to stay, protection and departure. The opposite mistake is just as risky, treating it as a paper where you merely lend your name. It is a document under which cost liability can actually be claimed.

It helps to know how the money flows. If a guarantor does not perform and the state ends up bearing the cost, the Minister of Justice can exercise a right of reimbursement against the guarantor. The demand comes in writing with the basis for the amount, and you must pay within 15 days of receiving the notice.

If there is a concern that the guarantor will not pay, or the guarantee alone will not achieve its purpose, the guarantor can be made to deposit up to 3 million won per foreigner (as of October 2026, Article 90(4)). The deposit is used toward stay, protection and departure costs if the guarantor does not perform, and is returned when the obligation is performed or the purpose of the guarantee is considered achieved.

## How many years can the guarantee period run?

The maximum guarantee period is 4 years. The form carries the same wording, and its sample entry runs from the application date up to 4 years at most. Writing 5 or 10 years still gets you only 4.

The period is tied to the stay period as well. When permits are issued, the permit period cannot exceed the guarantee period on the form. So a short guarantee period can shorten the stay the foreigner receives. The Ministry guidance says K-point E74 can grant up to 2 years per grant, but if the guarantee period is shorter than 2 years, the 2 years cannot be granted, even when the employment contract is for 2 years.

On the other hand, if a stay is extended within the guarantee period, the form does not need to be submitted again. If the guarantee period has already run out, a new one may be required in some cases. Some visas for family of marriage migrants even specify a guarantee period of 3 years from the date of entry.

So deciding how many years to write is a bigger choice than it looks. A longer period helps the foreigner, but it also keeps you bound for longer.

## Which stays call for an identity guarantee?

Not every status requires one. The law says a guarantee can be required when it is considered necessary in connection with visa issuance, stay permits, protection of foreigners and the like. These are the typical cases in the Ministry guidance.

| Situation | What the guarantee involves |
|---|---|
| Visa for the purpose of living together in marriage | The inviting spouse becomes the identity guarantor of the invitee |
| Residence (F-2-3) visa for the spouse of a permanent resident (F-5) | Identity guarantee from the spouse in Korea |
| Change to Residence (F-2-2) for a minor foreign child of a Korean national | Identity guarantee from the parent holding custody |
| Permanent residence application | Identity guarantee submitted (exempt for some F-5 categories) |
| Extension for a person who entered to visit relatives | Identity guarantee if aged 20 or over |
| Points-based specialist work (E-7-4) | The employer acts as the guarantor |
| Arts and entertainment (E-6), E-6-2 only | Only this status takes an identity guarantee |

This is only a list of typical cases. Even within one status the requirement can be waived depending on the type of application, and the guidance notes that immigration offices may add or remove documents during review. Before signing, ask which application you are guaranteeing.

## Who can be a guarantor, and when can't you?

Basic eligibility works like this. You must have an address in Korea and show that you have the means to give the guarantee. A foreigner who acts as guarantor must hold an Alien Registration Card (외국인등록증), and the guarantee period cannot exceed the guarantor's own period of stay. If the foreigner belongs to an organization or body, the head of that organization is the guarantor unless there is a special reason, and in that case no separate proof of means is required. The form can also be waived for guarantees by government bodies and similar cases.

As for "who can never be a guarantor," I could not find a prohibition list in the law. There are provisions that restrict eligibility, though.

- If you failed to meet guarantee obligations within the most recent year counted from the application date, your eligibility may be restricted for up to one year.
- If the head of the organization, body or company is the guarantor and failed to meet obligations three or more times within the most recent year, that also triggers a restriction.

Your address and means are things you must prove, so decide before signing what materials will show them.

## You want to stop, or circumstances change

This is the question I hear most: how do I withdraw? Honestly, I could not find a provision or Ministry guidance that sets a method or form for withdrawal. Rather than writing a document on your own, the first step is to ask the immigration office with jurisdiction.

What the law does tell us is the effect. When a guarantor withdraws or there is no longer a guarantor, the Minister of Justice can cancel or change the foreigner's visa or stay permit. Withdrawal can therefore feed straight into the foreigner's status. How costs that have already arisen are settled after withdrawal is not written anywhere I found, so I will not state it as fact.

Changes of guarantor are regulated. If a guarantor who is a Korean national intends to leave Korea to settle abroad, the foreigner must designate a new guarantor, and the same applies when a foreign guarantor leaves. If you are planning to emigrate or leave, talk with the foreigner about this early.

## Check before you sign

1. Find out whose application it is and what type. The guarantee period may differ for a visa, an extension or a permanent residence application.
2. Set the guarantee period yourself. It cannot exceed 4 years, and a short one can shorten the permit period.
3. Prepare materials showing your address and means.
4. Think about whether you plan to leave Korea or emigrate. In that case a new guarantor is needed.
5. Keep a copy. If a reimbursement notice or an appearance request arrives, you can check the dates and contents right away.
