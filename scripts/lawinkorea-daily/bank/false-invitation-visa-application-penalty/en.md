When writing an invitation for a relative, friend or employee, some people think a small inaccuracy will do no harm. But false statements in invitation papers and visa applications are separately prohibited by the Immigration Act. This guide covers Article 7-2, the penalties and the effect on the invited person.

## Key points

- Inviting or arranging an invitation by improper means such as false statements or a false identity surety, and falsely applying for or arranging a visa or certificate of visa issuance, are prohibited (Article 7-2).
- Violation can bring up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (Article 94, item 3).
- The standard fine rises with the number of people invited (Enforcement Rule, Annex 7).
- An invited foreigner who entered through a false invitation can also face deportation (Article 46(1), item 2).

## 1. What is prohibited?

Article 7-2 says no one may do either of two things to bring a foreigner into Korea.

- **Invite a foreigner by improper means**, such as false statements or a false identity surety, or **arrange** such an invitation
- **Falsely apply for a visa or a certificate of visa issuance**, or **arrange** such an application

Inflating the inviter's job, income or relationship, or citing a contract or business that does not exist, may fall here. Each case depends on its facts, so nothing can be stated categorically.

## 2. Penalties and standard fines

Article 94, item 3 punishes a person who violates Article 7-2 with up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million. Prosecution needs a complaint from the office head (Article 101(1)), and the head may notify a penalty fine equivalent to a criminal fine once guilt is confirmed (Article 102(1)). The Enforcement Rule Annex 7 standard amounts are below.

| People invited | Standard fine |
|---|---|
| 1 | KRW 10 million |
| 2 | KRW 12 million |
| 3 | KRW 14 million |
| 5 | KRW 18 million |
| 10 | KRW 28 million |
| 11 or more | KRW 30 million |

Amounts can be reduced or increased within one half for age, circumstances, motive, result and ability to pay (Enforcement Rule, Article 86(2)). If the offense is considered punishable by imprisonment or more, a complaint can be filed without a penalty notice (Article 102(3)). A notice must be paid within 15 days (Article 105(1)), and after payment you are not punished again for the same matter (Article 106).

## 3. Effect on the invited foreigner

Article 46(1), item 2 makes a foreigner who violated Article 7-2, or who entered through a false invitation under that article, subject to deportation. A permanent resident is not deported except in the cases the law lists (Article 46(2)). A person who wants to leave at their own expense can be given a departure order (Article 68(1), item 1), so see the [voluntary departure guide](/en/blog/foreigner-voluntary-departure-2026) and the [entry ban removal guide](/en/blog/entry-ban-removal). Grounds and length of any entry restriction depend on the case.

## 4. How is it investigated?

To prevent illegal entry by false invitation, immigration officers may visit people or businesses that arrange or broker invitations or international marriages, ask questions and request materials (Article 81(2)). Refusal without good reason is not allowed (Article 81(4)), and refusing or avoiding a request for books or materials can bring a fine of up to KRW 1 million (Article 100(2), item 3). If you are asked to appear, organize the facts and materials first, as in the [offense review guide](/en/blog/immigration-offense-review-guide).

## 5. A checklist before and after preparing invitation papers

1. **Verify each statement with evidence.** Check whether job, income, relationship and purpose can be supported by documents.
2. **Do not rely only on a broker's explanation.** Arranging is also prohibited, and the applicant remains responsible for the papers.
3. **Correct any error before submitting.** If already submitted, ask your office how to correct it.
4. **If you get a summons or fine notice, check the deadline first.** A fine notice runs 15 days from receipt.
5. **Review the invited person's stay plan separately.** Deportation and entry restrictions differ case by case.
