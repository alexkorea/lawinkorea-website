The F-4 visa is known for having few work limits, which is exactly why people get caught. The short answer: even on F-4 you cannot do unskilled labor, work at gambling venues or entertainment bars, or take certain service and sales jobs that the Ministry of Justice lists separately. If it comes out that you worked in one of these, you may face a fine, a denied extension of stay and other disadvantages. If you have already worked, the first job is to work out exactly which rule is in play.

## Key points

- F-4 holders can work freely except for unskilled labor, acts against good morals, and jobs that must be restricted to protect the public interest or the domestic job market.
- Whether a job is restricted is decided by the Ministry of Justice notice list. Cleaning, security, parcel and food delivery and unskilled construction work are typical examples, while kitchen helpers, housekeepers and unskilled farming are marked allowed even though they sound similar.
- In depopulation-area regional visa pilot regions, unskilled labor opens up, but only within the metropolitan or provincial area concerned.
- A violation can bring offense handling, a fine and a denied extension, and an accumulated fine record can limit F-4 grants later.

## 1. Which jobs are restricted?

The law lifts most activity limits for F-4 and blocks only three things: unskilled labor, acts against good morals or public order, and jobs that must be restricted for the public interest or to protect the domestic employment order.

The Enforcement Rule defines "unskilled labor" as work requiring simple, routine physical labor, meaning the fields of unskilled workers in the Korean Standard Classification of Occupations. The specific jobs are in the Ministry of Justice notice. The notable ones:

- Moving workers, mail carriers, parcel couriers, quick-service and delivery-agency riders, restaurant delivery workers, regular milk or newspaper deliverers
- Building cleaners, street and public-space cleaners, garbage and recyclables collectors
- Apartment security guards, building patrol guards, parking attendants, ticket checkers
- Flyer distributors, meter readers, gas inspectors, shoe shiners, hand launderers and simple pressers

On the morals side, the notice lists gambling venues, working as entertainment staff at entertainment bars, and workplaces among regulated custom businesses that offend good morals. The third category, public-interest restrictions, includes foot-care therapists, bath attendants, wedding staff, karaoke service staff, PC-room and video-room staff, golf caddies, alcohol service staff and street or mobile vendors.

Many jobs do not appear by name in the notice list, so use the Ministry of Justice table below alongside it.

## 2. How confusing jobs split

The ministry has a table of "commonly confused restrictions". Copying only the F-4 marking for people living outside the pilot regions:

| Allowed | Restricted |
|---|---|
| Product sorter, other manufacturing unskilled worker | Construction unskilled worker |
| Housekeeper, child-care helper | Moving worker |
| Fast-food preparer, kitchen helper | Parcel courier, food delivery worker |
| Unskilled farming, forestry and fishing worker | Building cleaner, building security guard |
| Rental-product visiting inspector | Wedding staff, golf caddie |
| Hotel service staff, other lodging service staff | Street and mobile vendor |
| Food service staff, beverage service staff | |

Restaurant work is the most common point of confusion. In the same restaurant, kitchen helper and food service staff are marked allowed while food delivery worker is marked restricted. Look at what you actually do, not the shop's name.

Note that even an allowed job still requires any qualification that Korean law demands for it.

## 3. When the restriction is lifted

There are two cases.

**Depopulation-area regional visa regions.** An F-4 holder who has a residence in a city, county or district designated by the Minister of Justice for the regional-specialized visa program, or who works within the metropolitan city or province that contains it, is not subject to the unskilled-labor and public-interest limits. Venues that offend good morals remain off limits. The catch is geography. Working in a different metropolitan or provincial government area than the recommended region is treated as a violation of the F-4 employment limits, with offense handling, and for a regional-specialized compatriot (F-4-R) it is a breach of permit conditions that the ministry says can lead to denial of extension or even cancellation of status.

**Former H-2 workplaces.** Since February 2026 the visit-employment (H-2) and overseas Korean (F-4) tracks have been merged into F-4. If you want to keep working at a workplace that you reported under H-2 after switching to F-4, apply for permission to engage in activities outside your status of stay in advance. Whether you may simply carry on without permission has to be checked separately, so if you are still at the same workplace after the change, confirm first whether permission is required.

## 4. What can happen if you violate the limits?

These are the disadvantages that appear directly in the source texts.

- **Offense handling.** Ministry guidance says breaking the F-4 employment limits makes you a target of offense handling. If an investigation produces firm evidence of an offense, the immigration office can serve a written notice to pay a fine equivalent to a criminal fine, and if the offense appears to warrant imprisonment it files a complaint straight away. Which way it goes depends on how serious the violation is.
- **Denied extension.** The guidance says an F-4 holder who violated the law may have an extension of stay restricted, and the F-4 non-employment pledge contains a statement that you were told you may face a fine and denial of an extension.
- **Breach of permit conditions.** A regional-specialized compatriot (F-4-R) who breaks permit conditions faces measures such as cancellation of status, restraint on extension and offense handling. The law lets the Minister cancel or change a permit for breach of conditions, may summon the person to hear their opinion, and must notify the reason, date and place at least 7 days before the hearing date. A person whose permit is cancelled can be given a departure order.
- **Limits that follow later.** If fines imposed for Immigration Act violations total KRW 7 million or more, you are a restricted person for F-4 grants and the like for 3 years from the day you paid the fine that took the total to that level. If a Korea Volunteer Center record shows at least 100 hours of volunteering over at least 6 months, that period shortens to 1 year. Total criminal fines of KRW 7 million or more carry the same 3-year restriction (as of October 2026, Ministry of Justice residence manual).

The employer's liability is a separate matter. General rules on hiring foreigners are covered in [Penalties for Illegal Employers](/en/blog/illegal-employer-penalty). From your side, even if the workplace said "F-4 can do anything", the pledge form has you stating that you will not take restricted jobs, so assume responsibility can come back to you.

General handling of illegal work and activities outside status is covered in [Handling of Unauthorized Activities](/en/blog/unauthorized-activity-disposition-2026) and [Illegal Employment Penalties](/en/blog/illegal-employment-penalty).

## 5. If you have already worked in a restricted field, in this order

1. **Identify exactly which occupation the work was.** Write down the real duties, not the job title (whether delivery was included, the scope of cleaning, whether patrol was part of guarding), and match them to the table and the notice list. If it was an allowed job, there may be no problem at all.
2. **Check the regional exception.** Confirm whether your residence and workplace fell inside a pilot region and inside the same metropolitan or provincial area.
3. **Move to an allowed job.** Continuing a restricted job while explaining yourself weakens your position. If you changed jobs, keep the contract and pay records showing when.
4. **Collect proof of the work.** Employment contract, pay deposit records, health insurance enrollment records, and anything showing the period and form of work. This becomes the baseline if you have to explain.
5. **Check your extension timing.** An extension must be filed before the stay period ends, so do not miss the expiry date even while under investigation.
6. **If you receive a fine notice or a summons, check the deadlines first.** A fine notice must be paid within 15 days of receipt, and if it is not paid in that period the office must file a complaint (it does not if you pay before the complaint).
7. **If you have an H-2 history, see whether permission for activities outside your status applies.**
