If you have received a notice that your visa extension or status change was denied, and you cannot realistically leave by the date written on it because you can't get a flight or you are ill, you can apply for a **deferral of the departure deadline**. If your permitted stay itself is about to end and you can't leave right away, the fitting tool is an **extension of stay for departure**. The names sound alike, but who can use each one, and the form, are different. Here is when each applies, how to apply, and how the period is treated in later reviews.

## Key points

- A **deferral of the departure deadline** is for people given a deadline through a denial notice, a departure recommendation or a departure order who cannot meet it for unavoidable reasons.
- An **extension of stay for departure** is for people whose permitted period of stay is ending and who cannot leave because there is no transport or for another unavoidable reason.
- Both are applied for with documents showing the reason, and the office decides. Applying does not mean automatic approval.
- The granted period is often not counted as stay for status-change and permanent-residence reviews, and status changes can be restricted while it runs.

## 1. Where does the departure deadline come from?

To understand a deferral, you first need to know where the deadline comes from. When the office refuses to grant, change or extend a status, it notifies you on a prescribed form, and that form must state a departure deadline within 14 days of the date of issue. The Minister of Justice can instead use the expiry date of the already permitted stay as the deadline when needed, and where the person is allowed to remain in the status already held, the deadline may be left blank.

A departure recommendation allows a deadline within five days of issue, and for a departure order the deadline is set under a Ministry of Justice ordinance. With periods this short, real-life problems such as flights, health and moving out run into the deadline often.

## 2. Deferral of the departure deadline: when you can get it

Three groups are covered: people who received a notice denying the grant of a status, people who received a departure recommendation, and people who received a departure order. The office may defer the deadline when it is **clear that the person cannot leave in time because there is no ship or other transport, or because of illness or another unavoidable reason**.

You apply by submitting the departure deadline deferral application form, with materials explaining the reason, to the competent immigration office. If you are in a detention facility, the head of that facility is the point of contact.

The word "clear" matters. "I have circumstances" is not enough. You need **documents that show from the outside why you cannot leave**, such as a medical certificate, a hospitalization record or evidence that no flight is available.

The length depends on the reason. Where there is no transport, the deferral can last until a date set separately by the Minister of Justice after considering the expected number of departing people and transport conditions. In other cases, such as illness, it can last until the reason ends. So there is no fixed number of months; it follows the reason.

## 3. Extension of stay for departure: when to use it

This is for a person **whose permitted period of stay is expiring**. The law gives two situations.

- A registered foreigner who has finished the activities of their status and wants to stay briefly for domestic travel or similar purposes
- A person who cannot leave because there is no ship or other transport, or for another unavoidable reason

You apply with the stay extension application form and materials explaining the reason. If the extension would exceed 30 days, the approval of the Minister of Justice is required, so it can take longer. If this applies to you, move early rather than waiting for the last day.

It also differs from an ordinary extension. An ordinary extension is for staying on and continuing activities; this one is an extension **in order to leave**. That is why later reviews look at it differently.

## 4. The two systems side by side

| | Deferral of departure deadline | Extension of stay for departure |
|---|---|---|
| Who | A person given a departure deadline by a denial notice, recommendation or order | A person whose permitted stay is expiring |
| Reason | No transport, or illness or another unavoidable reason makes leaving in time clearly impossible | Finished activities and staying briefly for domestic travel, or no transport or other unavoidable reason |
| Form | Deferral application form + supporting materials | Stay extension application form + supporting materials |
| Length | If no transport, until a date set by the Minister of Justice; otherwise until the reason ends | Over 30 days needs the Minister's approval |

## 5. How the period is treated in later reviews

This is the part people miss most in practice. Because the period was granted for leaving, later reviews may **not treat it as normal stay**.

- **Change to Other Long-term (F-2-99):** both the extension-for-departure period and the deferral period are subtracted when adding up length of stay, along with illegal stay periods and departure recommendation or order periods. People in either period are also listed among those for whom status changes are restrained.
- **Permanent residence:** when counting time in Korea, the extension and deferral periods are **entered as time spent outside Korea**.
- **Change to Spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1):** a person who received an extension of stay for departure generally cannot change status, and it is also written that a person with a deferral is not eligible. In that case the person has to leave and enter again with a visa issued by a Korean mission abroad. There is an exception where a change in Korea is judged unavoidable, for example because of pregnancy, childbirth or raising a child born within the marriage, and the change is allowed after review.
- **Marriage-breakdown change (F-6-3):** a person who received an extension of stay for departure falls among those restricted.
- **Accompanying family changes for Regional Talent (F-2-R) and Region-specific Skilled Worker (E-7-4R):** people with an extension for departure or a deferral are restricted.

In short, a plan like "I'll get a deferral first and change status in the meantime" rarely works. If your real goal is to stay in Korea, check first whether another route exists before using these systems.

## 6. Refugee applicants and deferral: one point

For a Korean-born child of a refugee applicant (G-1-5) to receive Other (G-1-99) status, the parent must be staying legally, and it is written that a parent who is a refugee applicant **under a departure deadline deferral is not covered**. Separately, a person in Labor Office mediation over unpaid wages (G-1-4) is extended within a six-month range, while a person under a departure-period deferral is extended within a three-month range. The same status can be handled by different standards for someone under a deferral.

## 7. What happens if you miss the deadline?

If you neither apply nor leave, the situation changes.

- **Staying on after the permitted period ends without an extension permit:** the law requires an extension permit before the period ends, and continued stay in breach can mean up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (as of October 2026, Article 94, item 17). It is also on the list of grounds for deportation. In practice this often turns into a penalty fine, which is covered in [our guide to penalty fines for missing an extension](/en/blog/overstay-penalty-extension-missed).
- **Not leaving by the date in a departure order:** a deportation order must be issued without delay, and a deposited performance bond (which can be set at up to KRW 20 million) can be forfeited in whole or in part (as of October 2026, Article 68(3) and (4)).
- **Not complying with a departure recommendation:** you can become subject to a departure order.

The pros and cons of leaving voluntarily are covered in [our voluntary departure guide](/en/blog/foreigner-voluntary-departure-2026), so they are left out here. The one point that matters is to apply **before the deadline passes, not after**, together with your supporting documents. If the deadline has already passed, do not delay; check your situation with the office. What follows when the departure deadline on a denial notice is missed depends on the notice and your circumstances, so it is hard to say in general terms.

## 8. Order of steps before you apply

1. **Write down the date on your notice or permit.** First separate whether it is a departure deadline or the expiry of your stay, because that decides which form you use.
2. **Turn the reason you can't leave into documents.** A medical certificate, hospital records, proof that no flight is available, or documents about family circumstances.
3. **Submit to the competent office before the deadline passes.** If the extension is likely to exceed 30 days, allow for the extra approval time.
4. **Review your later stay plans separately.** As seen above, there can be effects on status changes and permanent residence.
