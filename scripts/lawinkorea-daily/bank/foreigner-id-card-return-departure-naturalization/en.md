Some people have received their naturalization certificate and finished resident registration, yet the alien registration card is still in a drawer. Others lose a family member in Korea and do not know what to do with the card. In short, you must return the card when you leave Korea, when you become a citizen and when the cardholder dies, and failing to do so can lead to a fine.

## Key points

- The alien registration card must be returned **on departure**, **on becoming a citizen** and **on death** (Immigration Act, Article 37(1) and (2)).
- A person who became a citizen through naturalization returns it within 30 days of completing resident registration. In the case of death, the deadline is 30 days from the day the death became known (Enforcement Decree, Article 46(2)).
- People who leave temporarily with a re-entry permit, and similar cases, do not have to return the card on departure.
- Breaching the return duty can bring a fine of up to KRW 1,000,000, and Annex 2 of the Enforcement Decree sets amounts from KRW 100,000 to KRW 1,000,000 by number of violations (as of November 2026, Article 100(2), item 1, Enforcement Decree Annex 2)

## 1. Do you have to return the card when leaving Korea?

Article 37(1) requires a registered foreigner to return the card to an immigration officer on departure. You do not have to in the following cases.

1. You hold a re-entry permit, leave temporarily and plan to re-enter within the permitted period.
2. You hold a multiple-entry visa, or are a national of a country exempt from re-entry permits, leave temporarily and plan to re-enter within the permitted period of stay.
3. You hold a refugee travel document, leave temporarily and plan to re-enter within its validity period.

In other words, if you plan to come back to Korea and fit one of these three cases, you can take the card with you. To check whether you need a re-entry permit, see our guide on [re-entry permits and exemptions](/en/blog/reentry-permit-exemption-guide).

A card returned on departure leads to notification of the departure and the procedure to cancel the registration details. The registration details may also be cancelled if you do not re-enter within the re-entry permit period after leaving (or, if exempt, within the exempted period or the permitted period of stay) (Article 37-2(1)).

## 2. What is the deadline for naturalization or death?

Article 37(2) requires the card to be returned when a registered foreigner becomes a citizen or dies, and Enforcement Decree Article 46(2) sets the timing and method.

| Situation | When to return | Who returns it |
|---|---|---|
| Became a citizen (naturalization, etc.) | Within 30 days of completing resident registration | The person, spouse, parents, etc. |
| Death | Within 30 days of learning of the death | Spouse, parents, etc. |
| Became exempt from registration (diplomats, etc.) | When receiving permission for a change of status | The person |

In the case of death, a document proving the death, such as a medical certificate or post-mortem report, must be attached to the card. After the procedure is finished, the returned card is destroyed, and the city, county or district office and the town or neighborhood office are notified of the cancellation of the alien registration (Enforcement Decree, Article 46(3) and (6)).

## 3. What happens if you do not return it?

A person who violates Article 37 is subject to a fine of up to KRW 1,000,000 under Article 100(2), item 1. The individual standard in Annex 2 of the Enforcement Decree is KRW 100,000 for the 1st violation, KRW 300,000 for the 2nd, KRW 500,000 for the 3rd and KRW 1,000,000 for the 4th or more. The count increases if you received a fine for the same violation within the last 3 years (as of November 2026, Article 100(2), item 1, Enforcement Decree Annex 2)

The head of the immigration office may reduce or increase the amount by up to one half, taking the violator's circumstances into account (Enforcement Decree Annex 2, general standards). The Minister of Justice may also waive the fine after considering age and circumstances, the motive and result of the violation, ability to pay and similar factors (Article 100(5)).

## 4. Other duties that are easy to confuse with returning the card

- **Temporary retention:** If necessary for the interests of the Republic of Korea, the head of the immigration office may temporarily keep the card of a departing foreigner (Article 37(4)). If you re-enter within the permitted period, you must collect it within 15 days, and if you do not re-enter within the period, the card is deemed returned (Article 37(5)).
- **Loss or damage:** If you lost the card, the issue is reissuance, not return. We explain how in our guide on [carrying, losing and reissuing the card](/en/blog/foreigner-id-card-carry-lost-reissue).
- **Permanent resident card:** It must be reissued before its validity period ends. See our separate guide on [permanent resident card validity](/en/blog/pr-card-validity-renewal-penalty).
- **Changes in details:** If registered details change, for example when you renew your passport, you must report the change within 15 days. See our guide on [change reports](/en/blog/alien-registration-info-change-15days).

## 5. If the deadline has already passed

1. **Sort out the dates of naturalization, death or departure.** First confirm the date the deadline starts from, such as the day resident registration was completed or the day you learned of the death.
2. **Ask the competent immigration office how to return the card.** Also ask how to submit the card and the documents that confirm identity.
3. **In case of death, gather the proof.** A document proving the death, such as a medical certificate or post-mortem report, is required.
4. **If you received a fine notice, explain your situation.** It helps to set out truthfully why you were late and whether the card has been returned.
